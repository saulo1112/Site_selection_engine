# Study area selection

> Replicates the AOI-check approach of the EUDR project: the study city is
> decided using real data, not by eye. Available signal is measured on two fronts:
> (1) density of the reference brand (D1 Stores) in OpenStreetMap, and (2)
> availability of demographic/socioeconomic data from DANE and municipal
> portals.

## 1. Objective of the check

The look-alike model (v2/v3) needs, per candidate city, enough positive hexagons
(with D1 presence) so that, after applying **spatial CV** (exclusion of
spatial buffers in v3), viable positives remain in the training and
test sets. If a city has few labeled D1 stores, the spatial split may leave
too small or imbalanced a sample. That is why the check measures the D1 signal
**before** committing to a city.

## 2. Methodology

- **Boundary**: each city is delimited by its **OSM administrative boundary**
  (municipality/district relation), not a bounding box, to obtain an honest count
  within the actual urban limits and avoid spillover from neighboring
  municipalities (e.g. Soledad in the Barranquilla metropolitan area).
- **Area resolution**: each city's relation id is resolved at runtime via
  Nominatim (with a hardcoded fallback id in `src/config.py` if Nominatim fails).
  The script logs the id actually used in each run.
- **Overpass queries** (`out count;`) per city:
  - `D1 (brand)`: `shop=supermarket` + `brand` matching D1/Tiendas D1.
  - `D1 (by name)`: cross-check via `name~"D1"` (captures mislabeled D1 stores
    lacking the `brand` tag).
  - `Ara`: `shop=supermarket` + `brand=Ara`, as a backup in case D1 were scarce.
  - `Total shop=*`: all store-type POIs, as a proxy for general OSM tagging
    density in the city (to avoid confusing "little D1" with "little mapped").
- **DANE/stratum sources**: verified manually via web search (not programmatic,
  since the census has no counting API equivalent to Overpass) — see §4.

Script: `src/selection/data_availability_check.py` (`uv run python -m src.selection.data_availability_check`).
Raw Overpass responses: `data/raw/overpass_<city>.json`.

## 4. Availability of DANE data and socioeconomic stratum

The National Population and Housing Census (CNPV 2018) and DANE's National
Geostatistical Framework (MGN) cover the **four cities uniformly** at the
city-block/census-sector level, via the geoportal:
`https://geoportal.dane.gov.co/geovisores/sociedad/cnpv2018-detallado/`. Available
variables: population, households, housing units — but **not stratum** (socioeconomic
stratum is not a census variable; it comes from municipal
stratification, whose availability and currency do vary by city).

| City | Stratum — source | Granularity | Observation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bogotá** | IDECA — "Estratificación Manzana Bogotá D.C." and "Estrato Socioeconómico" datasets (`ideca.gov.co`, `datosabiertos.bogota.gov.co`) | City block | Best documented and most downloadable of the 4 |
| Cali | IDESC / Datos Abiertos Cali (`datos.cali.gov.co`) — "Estratificación socioeconómica según estrato, por barrio" | Neighborhood (2015) | More aggregated (neighborhood, not block) and less recent |
| Medellín | GeoMedellín / MEData (`medata.gov.co`) — socioeconomic stratum layer + block-level statistics | Block/parcel | Good granularity, less standardized portal than IDECA |
| Barranquilla | "Panorama Urbano" (stratification certificate, mayor's office) | Parcel (via procedure) | No downloadable open dataset at block level confirmed |

Since the CNPV/MGN does not differ between cities, **the real differentiator in
demographic data availability is the stratum**, and there too Bogotá (IDECA) is
better positioned than the others.

## 5. Recommendation

**Study city: Bogotá.**

Justification:
1. **Sufficient D1 signal** — the only city with D1 ≥ 40 stores (129), guaranteeing
   viable positives in the look-alike classifier even after excluding spatial
   buffers in v3. The other cities (Cali: 6, Medellín: 38, Barranquilla: 12)
   are at risk of an insufficient or imbalanced sample after spatial CV.
2. **Better stratum availability** — IDECA offers the most granular (block-level)
   and best-documented stratification dataset of the four cities.
3. The local-qualitative-validation tiebreaker clause (Cali) **does not apply**:
   the numbers were not comparable, so there is no tie to resolve. This is
   documented as a finding, not a forced decision.

**Limitation to document.** Bogotá also has the largest total `shop=*`
(21569), so the D1/shops ratio (0.60%) is not the highest of the four
cities — Barranquilla (1.48%) and Medellín (1.40%) have a higher *proportion* of D1
relative to total mapped commerce. However, for the look-alike classifier the
limiting factor is the **absolute count** of positives available for training and
validating with spatial CV, not the relative proportion — which is why Bogotá prevails.

**Reconciliation note (129 vs. 166).** This city check uses a **strict**
Overpass query (exact `brand`) to have a uniform, comparable criterion
across the 4 candidate cities: 129 for Bogotá. Once the city is fixed, the
production pipeline (`config.PIPELINE_QUERY_D1`) uses a more permissive query
(`brand` **or** `name~"D1"`, to capture mislabeled stores lacking the `brand` tag)
and loads **166** D1 POIs in `data/raw/pois_d1.geojson` — the number that actually
enters the pipeline and the look-alike model. The difference (129 → 166) is a change in
recall criterion, not a data inconsistency; it does not affect the city
decision, which dominates by an order of magnitude under either number.
